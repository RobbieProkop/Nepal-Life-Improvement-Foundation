How we work

Three commitments that decide
which projects we take on.

  1. i

    Assisting those experiencing hardship

    Our focus is small-scale projects for individuals and families, prioritised by urgency and basic needs, alongside community development resources that create long-term skill and learning opportunities.

  2. ii

    Working directly with villagers

    We consult with villagers to determine how best to improve their lives in both the short and long term, and partner with community leaders to increase employment opportunities.

  3. iii

    A chance to give

    All donations will be greatly appreciated and wisely spent. Programme coordination is entirely volunteer-run, so money goes to local labour, materials and instructors.

Why we started

From earthquake relief in 2015
to a registered non-profit in 2019.

2015

Earthquake relief, before there was an organisation

Our founder Maury Mason had been helping the community of Pachabhaiya — a village 200 kilometres west of Kathmandu, near the city of Pokhara — since 2015. Those early contributions helped people recover from the earthquake damage and the economic hardship that followed.

Homes first, then the paths between them

The first projects rebuilt and repaired damaged homes. From there the work expanded into village infrastructure: pathways, stairs, and connecting homes to water and hydro.

2019

Incorporated in Canada

In May 2019, together with Robbie Prokop, we incorporated as a Canadian non-profit organisation. The operating principle did not change: community leaders bring us the project, and we fund the materials and the local labour that builds it.

2020

A wider base, more work

In 2020 Lilly Mason came on board and helped expand our fundraising base, which has enabled us to take on considerably more projects — including the community centre now underway.

Current focus

One building, a dozen
programmes, ongoing.

Pachabhaiya Community Development Centre

Local community leaders approached us with an opportunity to take over a large three-storey building that had recently been left vacant. We are converting it into a community resource centre for educational and skill development workshops, including a demonstration permaculture garden where locals can learn sustainable agriculture.

Weekly fitness classes and children's art classes are already running. Management and programme coordination is volunteer-based, but we provide income for local labour and workshop instructors.

Planned workshops

  • Permaculture garden
  • Bee keeping
  • Jewellery making
  • Literacy classes
  • English classes
  • Home repair & tools

Project ledger

Everything funded to date,
most recent first.

  1. 01

    General

    Pachabhaiya Community Development Centre

    A three-storey building converted to host educational and skill development workshops. Coordination is volunteer-run; local labour and instructors are paid.

  2. 02

    Health

    Medical treatment

    Urgent medical and dental care, including life-saving treatment for a two-year-old boy with a serious kidney ailment. Without government-supported healthcare, many cannot afford proper care.

  3. 03

    Health

    COVID-19 food drive

    A ton of rice, half a ton of lentils and 300 litres of cooking oil distributed through the village — enough to give many families two months of food security until the harvest.

  4. 04

    Infrastructure

    Construction and home repair

    New construction and necessary repairs on several houses damaged by the earthquake and severe monsoons: replaced roofs, new kitchens, strengthened walls and foundations.

  5. 05

    Infrastructure

    Pathway and stair restoration

    Improved pathways and new stairways across the village for safer mobility, especially for the elderly and children.

  6. 06

    General

    Craftsmanship tools

    Stone and woodworking tools purchased for local crafts people.

  7. 07

    Infrastructure

    Gumba Stupa roof

    Repairs to the roof of the Gumba Stupa Buddhist temple after monsoon damage. An important cultural and spiritual centre; most of the labour was donated by village volunteers.

  8. 08

    Infrastructure

    Window installation

    One family had endured monsoon season after monsoon season with no protection from insects, weather or snakes. New windows were purchased and installed.

  9. 09

    Health

    Sanitary facilities

    Every household in the village now has proper bathroom facilities.

  10. 10

    Infrastructure

    Landslide restoration

    Repairs to damage caused by landslides during the 2020 monsoon, plus reinforcement work on homes identified as at risk.
